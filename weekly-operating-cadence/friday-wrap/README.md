# Friday Wrap

A Friday-morning prompt that closes the week the Monday Prep opened.

This prompt sits in the [weekly operating cadence](../). It runs Friday after the daily brief is published, reads from the week's briefs plus Monday Prep and Wednesday Review, and produces three things: a trajectory narrative for the week, a weekly support synthesis, and Friday comms drafts that bookend Monday Prep.

## What it does

Three things the daily brief doesn't:

**Closes the loop the week opened.** A trajectory narrative comparing what Monday Prep planned to what actually happened — naming the biggest gap, biggest surprise, biggest win, and what carries into next week. The cadence's accountability layer.

**Sees the patterns daily resolution can't.** A daily brief sees individual customer escalations. Friday Wrap sees a 12-ticket pattern across the week, the rate at which it's accelerating, and what it tells you about a product gap. Weekly resolution surfaces signals daily resolution misses.

**Surfaces cross-functional signals product teams under-see.** Marketing, sales, and support each generate signals about the product that rarely surface in product channels. Friday Wrap's job is to find them before they become surprises.

## What's in this folder

- **[TEMPLATE.md](./TEMPLATE.md)** — the full prompt

Friday Wrap inherits its principles, data source mappings, and structural philosophy from the [daily brief](../daily-brief/). The daily brief's IMPLEMENTATION.md covers the patterns that apply to all four cadence prompts.

## When to add this to your cadence

Same window as Monday Prep — after two or three weeks of daily briefs. Friday Wrap and Monday Prep are the bookend pair; they work best added together. They each take about 10-15 minutes of additional time per week, and together they create the start-of-week and end-of-week structure that turns the daily briefs into a visible weekly arc.

Friday Wrap depends on the daily briefs accumulating, on Monday Prep being run earlier in the week (for the plan-versus-reality comparison), and on the cross-functional channel access being configured. Without those, the output is thin.
