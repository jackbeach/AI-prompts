# Wednesday Review

A mid-week strategic checkpoint. The only prompt in the cadence that explicitly zooms out from execution to ask whether work still ladders to strategy.

This prompt sits in the [weekly operating cadence](../). It runs Wednesday, reads from this week's daily briefs plus Monday Prep, and produces a strategic-resolution review that catches drift before it becomes a quarter-end surprise.

## What it does

Three things the other prompts in the cadence don't:

**Detects strategic drift mid-week, when correction is cheap.** Most strategic drift isn't dramatic — it's the slow accumulation of execution decisions that each made sense in isolation but together moved the work away from the strategy. By the time it's visible at quarter-end, correction is expensive. Wednesday Review catches it through a ladder audit that traces every active initiative back to a strategic goal.

**Pressure-tests the metrics being tracked.** Most teams measure what's easy to measure. Wednesday Review runs every actively tracked metric through a seven-type diagnostic — detrimental, out-of-reach, incomplete, pressure, inconsequential, nonsensical, incongruent — to surface metrics that are quietly corrupting decisions. This part of the prompt is the part most worth reading on its own; the diagnostic framework holds value beyond just running the prompt.

**Frames OKR trajectory as the leading signal.** A goal rated "at risk" for three weeks in a row is effectively "off track" — the trajectory is the signal, not the rating itself. Wednesday Review surfaces those patterns explicitly so they can be addressed while there's still time to course-correct.

## When to add this to your cadence

Last of the four. After the daily brief, Monday Prep, and Friday Wrap are all running smoothly — typically four to six weeks after first adoption.

Wednesday Review is the most demanding prompt in the cadence. It expects the reader to engage the frameworks rather than skim outputs. The historical pattern data the prompt depends on (multiple weeks of briefs, multiple Monday Preps to compare against, OKR trajectory across multiple reviews) takes weeks to accumulate. Adopting it earlier produces thin output, and thin strategic output is worse than no strategic output.

Once added, Wednesday Review takes 30-45 minutes of focused reading and revision. It's not a glance-and-act prompt the way the daily brief is. It's a strategic checkpoint that earns its time only when the reader genuinely pressure-tests the findings.
